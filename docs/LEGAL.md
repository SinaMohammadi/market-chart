## Not financial advice

market-chart draws whatever data an application gives it. It does not provide market data, analysis,
recommendations or signals. Indicators, drawing tools, price labels and statistics are mathematical
transformations of the input data: they can be wrong if the input is wrong, delayed or incomplete, and they do not

## Example data is synthetic

All data shipped with the project (`@market-chart/testing`, the documentation, the playground and the
benchmarks) is generated by seeded random walks. Preset names such as `BTCUSDT`, `EURUSD` or `ACME` in
`syntheticAssets` only select generator parameters that loosely resemble an asset class; the resulting prices are
**not** the prices of those instruments and must never be presented as real or exchange-verified market data.
The documentation and the demos label generated symbols with the `SYN:` prefix or the word "synthetic".

## Market data in your application

If you connect a real data provider (through `@market-chart/datafeed` or your own code), the provider's terms
apply to your application: licences for display and redistribution, exchange fees, delayed-data labelling,
attribution and restrictions on storing or exporting data. If your licence forbids exporting data or images, leave
out the export and screenshot actions:
`createChartUi(chart, { builtins: { exclude: ['chart.exportData', 'chart.exportImage', 'header.export', 'header.screenshot'] } })`.
Check these terms before you publish an application that shows real data.

## Trading interfaces

`@market-chart/ui` (symbol search, price actions, order ticket) and `@market-chart/trading` (orders, positions,
alerts and markers on the chart) are **user-interface components only**:

- They **never execute, route, modify or cancel orders** and contain no broker, exchange or payment code. They
  call your application's callbacks (`submit`, `onModify`, `onCancel`, `onClosePosition`) after an explicit user
  action, and they show what your application passes back. Your application is responsible for what happens in
  those callbacks.
- **The chart is not the source of truth.** Lines, labels, statuses and P&L text show the data your application
  provided, which may be stale (network delays, missed updates) or wrong. Reconcile with your broker or back end,
  show authoritative order and position state elsewhere in your interface, and do not let users rely on the chart
  alone.
- Confirmation is on by default in the order ticket, double submissions are refused and nothing is retried
  automatically. If you allow users to switch confirmation off, you are responsible for that choice.
- **Regulatory duties are yours.** If your application lets users place real orders, obligations such as
  licensing, client agreements, risk warnings, suitability or appropriateness checks, best execution, record
  keeping, market-abuse controls and accessibility requirements apply to your application and your organisation,
  not to this library.
- Examples and demos in this repository are **simulated**: an in-memory "broker" with random latency and
  rejections. Keep the "simulated" labelling if you publish them, and never connect them to a real account
  without your own review.

## Privacy

- **The packages collect nothing.** There is no telemetry, analytics or tracking, and no network request is made
  unless your application configures one: datafeed adapters (REST, WebSocket, SSE) and the REST storage adapter
  connect only to the URLs you pass. Symbol icons given as image URLs are loaded by the user's browser from the
  URLs you provide (with `referrerpolicy="no-referrer"`).
- **Storage.** Interface preferences (language, toolbars, favourite and recent symbols, trading display options)
  are kept in memory unless your application passes a storage adapter (local storage, IndexedDB or your server).
  If you store preferences or layouts on a server, they may be personal data under laws such as the GDPR: tell
  your users and handle them accordingly.
- **The documentation site** sets no cookies and loads nothing from third parties (its Content-Security-Policy
  allows only its own origin). It stores your theme and language choice in your browser's local storage, and the
  "Saved layouts" example stores its chart layout there; clear the site's data in your browser to remove them.
